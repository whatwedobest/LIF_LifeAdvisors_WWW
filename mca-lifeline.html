The MCA Lifeline™

The MCA Lifeline™ is Life Advisors’ consultative approach to helping business owners evaluate and restructure existing merchant cash advance obligations. It begins with the complete financial picture—not another advance.

What the MCA Lifeline™ is

The MCA Lifeline™ is a coordinated process for reviewing the business, its cash flow, and every active merchant cash advance. The work is to understand the current pressure, outline a business-focused strategy, and—if the owner chooses to proceed—begin restructuring existing obligations.

The MCA Lifeline™ is not a new merchant cash advance and is not presented as a loan that pays off existing MCAs. Available options and results depend on the contracts, funders, business finances, participation, and other circumstances.

Who this is for

Owners who used merchant cash advances to keep the business moving and now feel daily or weekly payments tightening around operations, payroll, inventory, or growth. It may also be appropriate when more than one MCA is drawing from the same revenue stream.

How the MCA Lifeline™ works

01 — Understand

Understand the Situation

We begin by learning how the business operates, what changed, which obligations are active, and where the immediate pressure is greatest.

02 — Develop a strategy

Develop a Business-Focused Strategy

We review the available information and outline a coordinated approach based on the business’s obligations, cash flow, priorities, and circumstances.

03 — Work toward resolution

Work Toward Resolution

When the business moves forward, Life Advisors begins working through the restructuring process and helps the owner navigate the path ahead.

Multiple MCAs

When several advances are active, each may draw from the same cash while carrying its own balance, schedule, and terms. Addressing only the newest obligation may leave the underlying pressure unchanged. A coordinated review maps the full picture first.

What you should understand before proceeding

You should understand the proposed process, costs, responsibilities, potential risks, and realistic expectations before making a decision. No responsible provider can guarantee a particular payment reduction, settlement, timeline, or outcome.
